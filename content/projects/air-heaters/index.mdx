# High-Temperature Air Heaters

Designed and manufactured stainless steel enclosures for four air heater modules as part of a high-temperature test rig at TESLAB. The system achieves temperatures up to 1500°F with 85 SCFM air flow at 60 psi.

## Project Background

Part of a research project at TESLAB (Turbomachinery & Energy Systems Lab) at UT Arlington to create a high-temperature test rig simulating jet engine turbine state conditions. The system requirements were demanding:
- **Temperature:** Up to 1500°F (815°C)
- **Flow Rate:** 85 SCFM net air flow
- **Pressure:** Up to 60 psi

No commercial off-the-shelf solution existed for these specifications, necessitating a fully custom design and fabrication approach.

## Design & Engineering

Using SolidWorks, I designed custom **SS 304 (stainless steel)** enclosures applying DFMA (Design for Manufacturing and Assembly) principles. The design prioritized ease of fabrication and assembly while meeting the extreme thermal and pressure requirements.

### Design Considerations
- **Material Selection:** SS 304 chosen for high-temperature oxidation resistance and structural stability
- **Thermal Expansion:** Accounted for differential expansion at operating temperatures
- **Pressure Containment:** Welded joints designed to maintain integrity at 60 psi
- **Serviceability:** Modular design allows individual heater element replacement
- **Mounting:** Integrated provisions for secure mounting to test rig frame

I created detailed technical drawings and a digital twin for validation before manufacturing, reducing the risk of costly fabrication errors.

## Manufacturing & Assembly

Collaborated closely with the university machine shop for sheet metal fabrication, bending, and welding operations. The manufacturing process involved:

1. **Sheet Metal Cutting:** Laser or waterjet cutting of SS 304 panels
2. **Bending:** Precision brake forming to achieve required geometry
3. **Welding:** TIG welding of seams for pressure-tight assembly
4. **Quality Inspection:** Dimensional verification against CAD models

I personally handled all electrical wiring, assembled the four heater modules, and integrated them with an off-the-shelf PID controller for precise temperature management.

## Results & Cost Optimization

Successfully implemented an **8.4 kW air heater system** distributed across **4 heater modules**. The system achieved target temperatures and flow rates required for turbine testing.

### Key Achievements
- **Performance:** Met all design specifications (1500°F, 85 SCFM, 60 psi)
- **Cost Savings:** 30% reduction in manufacturing costs by utilizing university machine shop
- **Reliability:** System operates continuously for extended test campaigns
- **Safety:** No incidents or pressure failures during operation

By outsourcing sheet metal fabrication to the university shop rather than expensive external vendors, I reduced manufacturing costs by 30% without compromising quality or performance.

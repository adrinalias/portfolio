Development of custom data acquisition system with hardware, firmware, and GUI for Formula SAE brake testing.

## Project Overview

As part of a 6-person Formula SAE team, I developed the complete data acquisition system from scratch. The goal was to measure brake performance characteristics and validate manufacturer claims about coefficient of friction at various temperatures.

The rig uses a motor to spin a brake assembly while measuring torque, speed, and temperature to enable data-driven design decisions for improved lap times.

## Design & Mechanical Integration

Developed a compact, table-top test rig to characterize brake performance, avoiding the footprint and cost of room-scale inertial dynos.

### Key Components
- **Steady-State Testing Rig:** Shifted from traditional inertial flywheel testing to steady-state analysis. This allowed for more granular mapping of CoF vs. Temperature without the need for massive mechanical energy storage.
- **Rotor Specification:** Utilized **4140 steel rotors** to establish a baseline for Formula SAE track performance, identifying a significant delta compared to manufacturer-provided cast iron data.
- **Adjustable Actuator:** Designed a C-shaped actuator with a wing-nut adjustable arm to interface with various master cylinders.
- **Thermal Mapping:** Integrated a multi-point monitoring system:
    - **Embedded Thermocouples:** Placed within the brake pad corners for internal core temp.
    - **Adhesive Thermocouples:** Monitored caliper surface temp to prevent brake fluid boiling.
    - **Infrared (IR) Array:** Non-contact sensor mapped temperature distribution across the rotor radius.

## Mechatronics & DAQ Development

Led the development of a custom Data Acquisition (DAQ) system to replace prohibitively expensive commercial solutions.

### Signal Integrity & Hardware
1. **Frequency-to-Voltage Conversion:** Integrated a TI chip to offload rotor speed square-wave processing. This decoupled measurement from MCU interrupt load, ensuring timing stability across all operating states.
2. **Calibration:** Validated speed-to-voltage linearity using a 100 Hz signal generator.
3. **Filtering:** Implemented hardware-level low-pass filters on load cell inputs to attenuate mechanical vibration from the torque arm.
4. **Design for Serviceability:** Final assembly utilized a structured protoboard layout rather than a custom PCB, ensuring the system could be easily modified or repaired by future mechanical leads without specialized ECAD tools.

### System Architecture
1. **Microcontroller:** SAME51 ARM Cortex-M4 handling real-time sensor fusion and CAN communication.
2. **Data Pipeline:** 20 Hz sampling rate feeding a real-time **Python-based GUI** for live visualization and CSV logging.

## Results & Validation

The rig successfully profiled multiple brake pad compounds, providing the Formula team with the first empirical CoF vs. Temperature curves in the team's history.

- **Empirical Validation:** Proved that manufacturer-provided data was often missing or inaccurate for track-specific temperature ranges.
- **Performance Impact:** Enabled the team to select brake compounds based on race duration (Endurance vs. Autocross), directly improving braking consistency.
- **Innovation Award:** Secured **2nd Place** at UTA Innovation Day 2025 for engineering excellence in budget-constrained environments.

## Technical Specifications

- **Microcontroller:** SAME51
- **Sampling Rate:** 20 Hz
- **Temperature Range:** 0-500°C (thermocouples)
- **Torque Measurement:** Load cell with custom amplification
- **Speed Detection:** IR reflective sensor
- **Communication:** Serial (USB) and CAN bus
- **Power Supply:** 12V DC with regulated 5V/3.3V rails

Led a high school team to the **World Finals** of the F1 in Schools competition, serving as Team Lead and Manufacturing Engineer. Our vehicle achieved the **Fastest Car Time** and the **Judge's Pick Award for Best Engineered Car**.

## Project Overview

F1 in Schools is an international STEM competition where teams design, manufacture, and race CO2-powered miniature cars. Our team, Mobius Racing, represented the nation at the World Finals after a successful national campaign.

## Role & Responsibilities

As **Team Lead**, I coordinated efforts across aerodynamics, structural design, and business planning while personally executing:
- **Vehicle Manufacturing:** CNC machining of balsa wood bodies and 3D printing of high-speed wheels.
- **Quality Control:** Dimensional verification to meet strict 0.05mm assembly tolerances.
- **Design of Experiments (DOE):** Optimized 3D printing parameters for wheel strength-to-weight ratios.

## Engineering & Optimization

Used **Fusion360** for vehicle modeling and **FEA** to validate structural integrity under high-impulse CO2 propulsion.

### Manufacturing Precision
- **Tolerancing:** Designed custom assembly fixtures to ensure bearing perpendicularity within **0.05mm**.
- **Friction Reduction:** Iteratively tested wheel alignments to minimize rolling resistance, a critical factor for the 20m track performance.
- **Aerodynamics:** Validated CFD predictions through iterative prototype testing, focusing on minimizing front-wing drag.

### 3D Printing DOE
Conducted a systematic study on layer adhesion for the wheel assemblies:
- **Optimal Settings:** 0.1mm layer height at 100% infill provided the necessary stiffness to withstand rotational forces without failure.

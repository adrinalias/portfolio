## Competition Overview

F1 in Schools is an international STEM competition where teams design, manufacture, and race miniature racecars powered by CO2 canisters. Our team, Mobius Racing, represented our high school at the National Finals.

The competition challenges students to apply engineering principles, manufacturing techniques, and project management skills to create the fastest possible car while meeting strict regulations and budget constraints.

## Design & Engineering

Used Fusion360 for wheel configuration design and elementary **FEA (Finite Element Analysis)** to validate structural integrity. Applied first principles engineering to optimize the car for maximum speed while staying within competition regulations.

### Design Constraints
- **Length:** Maximum 210mm
- **Width:** Maximum 55mm (must fit track)
- **Weight:** Minimum 50g (to prevent advantage from ultra-light designs)
- **Propulsion:** Standard CO2 cartridge only
- **Materials:** Balsa wood body (CNC machined)

### Optimization Goals
- Minimize aerodynamic drag
- Minimize rolling resistance
- Maximize structural stiffness (prevent flexing)
- Ensure bearing alignment for straight tracking

## Manufacturing Optimization

Conducted **Design of Experiments (DOE)** to control 3D printing variables and maximize layer adhesion and strength. This was critical for the wheel assemblies, which needed to withstand high rotational speeds without failure.

### DOE Variables Tested
- **Layer Height:** 0.1mm vs 0.2mm
- **Infill Density:** 50% vs 100%
- **Print Temperature:** 200°C vs 210°C
- **Print Speed:** 40mm/s vs 60mm/s

Results showed that 0.1mm layer height with 100% infill at 200°C provided optimal strength-to-weight ratio.

### Assembly Fixtures
Designed custom fixtures to ensure precise alignment of components during assembly. Verified dimensional accuracy to achieve correct interference levels for bearing installation.

Poor bearing alignment can cause the car to pull to one side, dramatically affecting race times. The fixtures guaranteed perpendicularity within 0.05mm tolerance.

## Testing & Validation

Performed iterative testing to validate **CFD (Computational Fluid Dynamics)** predictions and structural analysis. Fine-tuned the design based on test results to minimize drag and maximize straight-line speed.

### Testing Protocol
1. **Baseline Run:** Establish initial performance metrics
2. **Aerodynamic Modifications:** Test nose cone variants
3. **Weight Distribution:** Adjust component placement
4. **Wheel Alignment:** Fine-tune bearing positions
5. **Final Validation:** Confirm repeatable performance

Each design change was tested multiple times to ensure improvements were real and not statistical noise.

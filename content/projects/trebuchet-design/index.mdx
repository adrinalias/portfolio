Academic project designing and building a trebuchet using computer-controlled manufacturing. Achieved the target of launching a penny exactly 6 feet using only interference fits for assembly.

## Project Objective

Course project challenge: Design and build a trebuchet using computer-controlled manufacturing techniques. The objective was precise:
- **Projectile:** One penny
- **Counterweight:** Quarters only
- **Target Distance:** Exactly 6 feet
- **Assembly Constraint:** Interference fits only (no fasteners or adhesives)

This project combined mechanical design, precision manufacturing, and practical engineering iteration to achieve the exact performance target.

## Design & Engineering

Designed the trebuchet in SolidWorks with a focus on **mechanical adjustability** to compensate for simulation variances.

### Hardware-Based Calibration
Initial SolidWorks simulations provided inconsistent results due to complex multibody dynamics and friction variables. To ensure the 6-foot target was met, I integrated physical calibration features into the hardware:
- **Variable Pivot Points:** Drilled multiple holes along the trebuchet arm, allowing for rapid adjustment of the effort-to-load arm ratio.
- **Angular Stopper:** Designed a 3D-printed arc with a variable stopper to precisely control the arm's rotation limit and the resulting launch angle.
- **Sling Release:** Fine-tuned the release pin angle to optimize the projectile's trajectory.

## Manufacturing & Assembly

The structural frame was laser-cut from plywood, adhering to a strict **no-fastener/no-adhesive** constraint. I developed a custom locking mechanism using **finger joints and locking teeth** to secure the vertical stabilizers.

### Tolerance & Kerf Compensation
To achieve reliable interference fits, I conducted iterative tests to determine the laser's **kerf (0.005")**. By compensating for this material loss in the CAD geometry, I ensured that every joint was:
- **Secure:** Required a press-fit to assemble, providing high structural rigidity.
- **Modular:** Capable of multiple assembly/disassembly cycles without material degradation.
- **Rigid:** The locking teeth effectively distributed the impulse forces from the 2lb counterweight.

## Testing & Validation

The iterative testing phase focused on mapping the relationship between pivot location, stop angle, and launch distance.

### Calibration Protocol
1. **Pivot Selection:** Identified the optimal hole for the target distance range.
2. **Angle Fine-Tuning:** Adjusted the 3D-printed stopper in 5-degree increments.
3. **Weight Balancing:** Verified the impulse from the quarters-filled counterweight.
4. **Final Accuracy:** Achieved the 6-foot target with repeatable precision through hardware overrides rather than relying on the initial theoretical models.

## Results

The final trebuchet successfully launched the penny to the target distance without breaking any design requirements. The project demonstrated effective use of:
- **DFMA Principles:** Design optimized for laser cutting and 3D printing
- **Simulation Validation:** CAD models predicted real-world performance
- **Precision Manufacturing:** Tight tolerances achieved through computer-controlled processes
- **Engineering Iteration:** Prototype testing drove design refinement

### Key Learnings
- Importance of tolerance analysis for interference fit assemblies
- Value of rapid prototyping for validating design assumptions
- Impact of manufacturing process selection on part quality
- Need for adjustment mechanisms when targeting exact performance specs

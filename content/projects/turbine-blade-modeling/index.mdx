Root-cause analysis and redesign of a high-speed rotor for an Air Foil Bearing test rig at TESLAB. Analyzed interference fit failure at 40,000 RPM and 750°C using Ansys.

## Project Context

As a Research Assistant at TESLAB (UT Arlington), I supported the development of a test rig simulating the extreme thermal and rotational conditions of a jet engine. The rig required a rotor spinning at **40,000 RPM** in environments reaching **750°C**.

### The Problem: Dynamic Imbalance
During high-speed testing, the system experienced excessive vibration. Initial attempts to balance the rotor were unsuccessful; the assembly would lose its balance immediately after operation. I was tasked with root-causing the failure and redesigning the rotor assembly.

## Root-Cause Analysis

I conducted a systematic investigation to identify the source of the dynamic instability:
1. **Machine Validation:** Verified the balancing equipment using a calibration mass to rule out sensor error.
2. **Interference Fit Study:** Suspected that the joint between the disc and the shaft was loosening at high speeds due to centrifugal expansion.
3. **Simulation (Ansys):** Performed a rotational study to analyze the radial stress at the contact point.

### Simulation Findings
The analysis confirmed that at the **40,000 RPM critical speed** and 750°C, the expansion of the disc reduced the contact pressure to near-zero, causing the rotor to lose balance.

## Redesign & Engineering

To resolve the fitment failure, I proposed a redesign focusing on mass distribution and thermal stability.

### Material Selection
Materials were selected based on their specific strength and **Coefficient of Thermal Expansion (CTE)**:
- **Stainless Steel (500°C side):** CTE ~17.0 µm/m·K.
- **Titanium (750°C side):** CTE ~8.6 µm/m·K. Utilizing Titanium's lower CTE prevented excessive expansion at the hottest zone, maintaining the interference fit.

### Design Optimization
- **Radius Reduction:** Decreased the disc radius to shift mass toward the center of rotation, significantly reducing centrifugal expansion.
- **Flange Integration:** Added a small-radius flange at the contact point to ensure the joint remained under compression at 40,000 RPM.
- **Inertial Matching:** Increased disc thickness to maintain the required inertial load for airfoil bearing testing.

## Outcomes

### Technical Results
- **Stability:** Redesigned rotor maintained balance throughout the full 40,000 RPM range at 750°C.
- **Validation:** Empirical data matched Ansys predictions, proving the efficacy of the flange-based interference fit.
- **Efficiency:** Eliminated vibration-induced damage to the air foil bearings, enabling consistent, repeatable test campaigns.

### Key Learnings
- **High-Speed Dynamics:** Understanding the nonlinear effects of centrifugal force on mechanical joints.
- **Extreme Environment FEA:** Using Ansys to simulate coupled thermal-rotational stresses.
- **Material Performance:** Selecting aerospace-grade alloys based on thermal expansion and yield strength at 1000°F+.
